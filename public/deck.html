OPENSTATE

A household supply company run by six AI agents. Phones are its eyes. People just talk to it.

Track: Commerce, Retail & Consumer (inventory, logistics), with a Health & Care use: "did I already take my pills?"

The problem

Shared spaces leak. In a house, a dorm, a clinic store room or a small shop, nobody knows what is left, who took the last one, or when something went off. People write lists on fridges and forget them. Elderly people and anyone on daily medication lose track of whether they already took it today.

The information exists: it is visible to anyone standing in front of the shelf. OPENSTATE turns phones into observers that keep watching, and a team of agents into the staff that acts on what the phones see.

The company: six agents, one mission

Each agent has a role, a character, a Masky face and voice, and its own tools. They talk to each other in a BAND chat room that humans can read.

MiraGeneral Manager

Reads each request, picks the colleague for it, reports the business value.

IrisInventory Coordinator

Knows what is on every shelf right now, who took what and when.

TheoRecords Keeper

Keeps the long-term memory: facts, past sightings, the household's vocabulary.

NovaResearch Analyst

Looks things up outside the house in a sandboxed computer: shelf life, recalls, prices. Runs analyses over usage data.

RaviProcurement & Operations

Acts: shopping lists, alerts, reminders, emails, the ops pipeline. Never without being asked.

SolFront Desk

Talks to the household by voice. One or two spoken sentences from the colleagues' reports.

Demo flow

  1. Register a place. Observer 01 points a phone at the snack table: "Hey World, remember this." Iris stores the place, its reference photo and its counted contents.
  2. Someone takes an item. Observer 02 picks up a protein bar. The dashboard recognises the table, sends the episode, and the count drops by one, credited to whose camera saw it.
  3. Ask the company. "Hey World, what are we out of?" Mira delegates: Iris reports the counts, Theo the history, Nova the pace. Sol answers aloud in Masky's voice.
  4. Act. "Add what we're out of to my shopping list." Ravi runs the n8n workflow; the items land in Google Tasks. The ops pipeline on RocketRide carries the low-stock notice.
  5. Research in a sandbox. "Is there a recall on protein bars?" Nova boots a Tenki VM, fetches the sources, and reports with a citation.
  6. Watch them work. Every message between agents appears on the dashboard and in the BAND room, as it happens.

Business value and growth

Who it serves

Households and shared homes first: fewer wasted trips, less food thrown out, and a calm answer to "did I take my pills?" Then the places with the same problem and a budget: care homes, student housing, clinics' supply rooms, small shops.

How it earns

A subscription per household or per site. Costs are a few model calls per question and per shelf change; the camera work runs on the laptop and the phones.

How it grows

Every registered place is a data point about how fast things run out. That becomes reorder automation, supplier integrations through n8n and Kylon, and, for care settings, a record a family can trust.

How it keeps people safe

Agents act only when asked. "Ignore this" pauses the memory for five minutes and nothing is stored. The product spec is enforced on every pull request.

What works today, honestly

Real

Counting items on a registered place from phone video; noticing a removal and who took it; answering from stored state from any phone; long-term memory on Atlas; the six-agent delegation loop with a visible transcript; tool calling with sandboxed research; workflow actions through n8n; status checks for every sponsor module.

Depends on keys at demo time

Masky voices and faces, the BAND room mirror, RocketRide pipeline runs, Kylon actions and Paritok compression each switch on when their key is present. Without a key the module reports "not configured" and the company carries on.

Known limits

An update after a shelf change takes several seconds, most of it the vision model. Names for similar small items can drift between looks. Deep checks spend sponsor credits.
